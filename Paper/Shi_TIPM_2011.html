A Numerical Investigation of Transpiration Cooling with Liquid Coolant Phase Change
Tier 3 — 미등록
J. X. Shi, J. H. Wang
Transport in Porous Media, Vol. 87, No. 3, 2011, pp. 703-716 · DOI: 10.1007/s11242-010-9710-9 · ISSN 0169-3913
논문 요약
This paper develops a numerical model for transpiration cooling in which a liquid coolant injected into a porous matrix undergoes phase change. The novelty is coupling the two-phase mixture model (TPMM) with the local thermal non-equilibrium (LTNE) assumption, so that fluid and solid energy equations are solved separately while still avoiding explicit interface tracking through an enthalpy formulation. Using pure water as coolant in a one-dimensional finite-volume solution, the authors study how coolant mass flow rate, heat flux, solid thermal conductivity, porosity, sphere diameter, and operating pressure shape the temperature and saturation fields. A key finding is an inverse phenomenon: inside the two-phase region the coolant (fluid) temperature can exceed the solid temperature, which the LTE-based TPMM cannot capture. They also show that higher hot-surface pressure degrades cooling because the latent heat of water decreases with pressure.

Introduction — 연구 동기 (클릭하여 자세히)
Two-phase flow with phase change in porous media has traditionally been handled by the separate phase model (SPM), which is impractical because of interface tracking and many coupled nonlinear equations, motivating the two-phase mixture model (TPMM). The authors note that transpiration cooling has gained attention for scramjet combustors, hypersonic leading edges, turbine blades, and rocket nozzles, but most studies used gaseous coolant and few addressed liquid-coolant phase change. They argue that the LTE assumption underlying the TPMM is unverified for two-phase problems, and propose a numerical study of liquid-coolant transpiration cooling using an LTNE-TPMM.
Related Works — 관련 연구 (클릭하여 자세히)
The paper situates itself against two model lineages: the impractical SPM and the more convenient enthalpy-based TPMM, the latter widely used for two-phase porous-media flow. On the physics side it reviews phase-change transpiration-cooling studies on stability, temperature and pressure variation, and conductivity/sphere-diameter effects, noting all prior work assumed coolant remained cooler than the solid. It also references the LTE-versus-LTNE debate, where the LTE error had only been quantified for single-phase conditions.
Method — 방법론 (클릭하여 자세히)
The physical model is a one-dimensional porous matrix of thickness L exposed to heat flux Q on its hot upper surface, with liquid coolant injected from a reservoir at temperature T_c and mass flow rate m. To avoid tracking the liquid/two-phase/vapor interfaces, the TPMM enthalpy formulation reduces mass, momentum, and energy to identical forms across all three regions, while the LTNE model splits the energy balance into separate fluid and solid equations coupled by a volumetric solid-fluid heat exchange term. Enthalpy and saturation replace fluid temperature, with piecewise relations (Table 1) mapping enthalpy to temperature and saturation, and closure relations supply permeability, capillary diffusion, relative mobilities, and boiling heat transfer.
Experiment — 실험 (클릭하여 자세히)
The study contains no physical experiments; the 'experiment' is a one-dimensional numerical simulation. The governing equations are discretized on uniform control volumes with staggered velocity grids, converged to a strict relative-change tolerance, and verified for grid independence at 50/100/200 nodes (100 adopted). Pure water at 1 atm provides the coolant thermal properties, and parametric sweeps over mass flow rate, heat flux, conductivity, porosity, sphere diameter, and pressure generate all results.
Discussion — 논의 (클릭하여 자세히)
The central discussion is the inverse temperature phenomenon: contrary to all prior phase-change transpiration-cooling studies, the fluid (coolant) temperature can exceed the solid temperature at the onset of the two-phase region, an effect attributed to boiling heat transfer and capillary enthalpy diffusion and captured only by the LTNE-TPMM. The authors also discuss why porosity and sphere diameter dilate the two-phase region (via higher capillary diffusion) and why pressure degrades cooling (latent heat drops with pressure). They repeatedly caveat that these numerical findings require experimental or micro-scale confirmation.
Conclusion — 결론 (클릭하여 자세히)
The authors conclude that the new LTNE-TPMM successfully models transpiration cooling with liquid-coolant phase change and reveals an inverse fluid-solid temperature phenomenon driven by boiling and capillary diffusion that the LTE-based TPMM cannot reproduce. Higher hot-surface pressure worsens cooling, and the two-phase region, its saturation, and the liquid temperature are all controllable through porosity, sphere diameter, conductivity, or coolant injection rate.
